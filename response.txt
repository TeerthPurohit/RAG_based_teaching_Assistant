That's a great question to start with! In the context of this course, machine learning in trading is primarily about using algorithms to make decisions in financial markets.

Professor Balch explains in video 164, "Overview," starting around 0:0.0, that hedge funds and other financial institutions utilize machine learning to create models that can predict future prices for stocks or other assets. It's about building systems that can learn from data to make better trading decisions.

More specifically, a significant part of how we approach ML in trading in this course is through Reinforcement Learning (RL). Think of it as teaching a computer agent to trade by letting it interact with the market. As discussed in "Mapping trading to RL" (video 198, around 0:5), we map the trading problem to an RL framework where:
  The environment is the market itself.
  The state includes market features, prices, and whether you're holding a stock.
  Your actions are things like buying, selling, or holding.
  The reward (or R) is the return you get for making good trades, as mentioned in "The RL problem" (video 197, around 214:5).

So, in essence, ML in trading here means using these algorithms, especially reinforcement learning, to learn optimal trading strategies by observing the market and receiving feedback (returns) on actions taken.

I recommend checking out:
  "Overview" (video 164) for the general introduction to ML in trading.
  "Mapping trading to RL" (video 198) and "The RL problem" (video 197) to understand how trading specifically fits into the Reinforcement Learning framework.
